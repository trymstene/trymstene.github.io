# THE HOMESTEAD FARM PLAN — The Three Roads of Plot 11

## 1. THE GAME IN THREE SENTENCES

Your homestead is a small farm where the animals always pay you, and caring for them is a bonus, never a chore — one tap fills every trough, and a hug teaches a hen your face. The land is small on purpose: you can only fit a few pens, so *which* animals you keep decides which of three long roads your farm is walking — rebuilding the house, knitting wool into future armor, or cooking food the whole world will feel. Nothing ever dies, nothing ever punishes you for being away, and the animal you love most learns your name and meets you at the gate.

## 2. A DAY ON YOUR FARM

You arrive, and the coop door swings open. Henrietta — the hen you named last week — is already walking toward the gate to meet you. The morning notice reads: "Henrietta left you two eggs. The cheese shelf ticked up one."

You stroll through the yard. Eggs are lying by the nest, a milk can sits by the goat. You just walk over them — no taps, they're yours. Today is wool day: the sheep stands there freshly shaggy, and you shear her with one tap. The bundle drops.

You tap the well. The bucket swings, every trough on the plot flips to full, and one hen shimmers. That was the whole obligation of the day — two seconds. Tomorrow everything pays double because you did it.

You tap Henrietta. She turns to face you, a little heart floats up. That was the hug.

Then one thing, your choice: drop today's goods at the gate stall and watch the coins land, or pour milk into the cheese machine, or lay a few more fence pieces toward the next pen. Then you leave — and the farm keeps working while you're gone. Ninety seconds, one hand, on your phone.

## 3. THE ANIMALS AND WHAT EACH ONE IS FOR

| Animal | What it gives | What that's for | Personality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hen** (7 colors) | An egg every day, sometimes a rare golden egg | Coins — the house fund — and eggs for cooking | Day one, free, and one follows you home before you buy anything |
| **Rooster** | No goods. He reads out the morning news, and keeps the yard running while you're away (your stored yield lasts 3 days instead of 2) | Absence insurance with a face | The alarm clock — the yard's loudest, cheapest employee |
| **Sheep** | Wool, in 4 colors, every third day | **The only source of armor material in the whole world** — wool becomes knitwear, knitwear later becomes cast metal armor | Visibly shaggy before shearing, visibly shorn after — the pack's one real transformation |
| **Goat** | Milk, from a small pen | The kitchen, early — cheap milk before you can afford the cow | The scrappy starter dairy |
| **Cow** | Lots of milk, and cheese through the cheese machine | Cooking, coins, and expedition food — the shelf fills over days like a slow oven | The big engine; needs the biggest pen you can build |
| **Dog** | Nothing sellable, ever. Only love — and one day it follows your banana into other areas | The companion. Petting it counts as your daily hug | The heart of the farm. Everyone will buy the dog |

**Not shipping at first, and why:** the **duck**, **pig**, and **rabbit** stay in the barn until they have a real job. Your own rule killed the bird collection for a reason: a species that only *exists* is filler, not fun. The pig's real-world jobs are meat (banned) and truffles (no art). The rabbit is pure cute. The duck comes back only if we build ponds ("do I dig a pond for the fancy egg" is a genuinely good decision — later). The **donkey** waits for The Cut's ghosts: its job will be holding your ore bag when a ghost spooks you. No ghosts yet, no donkey yet.

## 4. CARING FOR THEM

**Do you need to feed and water them to get anything? No.** An unfed hen still lays, forever. Care is never the rent — it's the multiplier. Feed and water are **one verb**: tap the well, every trough fills, everyone eats and drinks. Doing it means everything pays **double tomorrow**, and you see it immediately — a shimmer on one hen today. Skipping it costs you nothing but the bonus. The trough shows empty as a calm morning appointment, never a wound.

**The hug is real, but morale is turned upside down.** There is no sadness meter, no drooping animal, no guilt. Instead every animal has a **bond** that only goes up. One tap a day on the animal you like: it turns to you, a heart floats. At bond 3 you get to **name** it — earned, never bought. At bond 7 it walks to **meet you at the gate** when you arrive. At bond 12 it follows you around the yard. Bond never drops, and it even survives selling: sell the goat, buy a goat next month, Gunnar remembers you.

Come back after two weeks away and the screen looks exactly like one night away. The news says "Henrietta waited by the gate." Absence is narrated as being missed — never as damage. Total daily care: about five seconds, whether you own two hens or ten animals. **Which animals you keep changes what you earn, never what you owe.**

## 5. THE STRATEGY

Your land fits roughly four or five pens, and bigger animals need bigger pens. You cannot have a full hen yard AND a sheep flock AND the giant cow pen AND the house. That squeeze is the whole strategy — and you feel it by walking your own yard, not by reading numbers.

The choice bites three times: **week two** (your second pen: goat for the kitchen, sheep for the armor road, or more hens for pure coins?), **week three or four** (the cow pen is the biggest land commitment in the game — take it and you can't fit a second specialty), and **every few days after** (the stall only banks so much per day, so a big farm overflows — and the overflow pushes you toward cooking or the wool chest whether you planned to or not).

Real farms players will build: **the Wool Baron** (sheep-heavy, poorer in coins, but the only person who'll walk into the ghost pit in armor their own sheep grew), **the Kitchen Farm** (goat now, cow and cheese machine later — the friend everyone wants provisions from), **the Coin Farm** (hens plus cow, house up fastest). Pens are containers — swap animals freely, nothing is ever lost — so experimenting is safe. But your deepest bond lives on the animal you *kept*, so commitment quietly feels different from optimizing.

## 6. THE GOALS

Three roads, marked by worn wooden signs at your gate. You never pick one in a menu — the road you're on is simply what your pens imply. **Each sign only ever shows the next milestone that actually exists in the game right now.**

**The Lantern Road — the dream of a home.** Tent by day five, cabin in two weeks, the full house in a month or so, all paid for by eggs and milk. You're rebuilding the house on Plot 11 — the one nobody has lived in since 1999.

**The Threadneedle Road — the dream of armor.** Here's the lore: behind the park there's a boarded-up house, and under it, a pit. Something is down there. One day it opens — ore in the rock, and ghosts. Ore doesn't buy anything; ore *coats* things — bronze, silver, gold. But you can't cast bare skin. You need a base garment, and the only material in the world for that is **wool your sheep grew**, knitted into a padded cap or a quilted tabard. Until the pit opens, the knitwear is already a real wearable at the rave — the road pays before its ending exists. When the pit opens: your sheep's wool, your knitting, cast in bronze, worn into the dark. "The wool took the hit."

**The Chimney Road — the dream of the table.** Milk to cheese to the seven dishes, ending at the buff dishes — which finally become something you can *feel*: a packed lunch. A cheese board in your pocket absorbs one ghost-spook in a crumb explosion. Stew doubles what you carry out. The cook is the person every expedition needs.

Plus two ladders open to everyone: **the Golden Flock** (steady care permanently graduates an animal to its golden form — prestige bought purely with attention) and **the Companion** (the dog, at full bond, walks with you everywhere — the one farm reward every other area shows off for free).

## 7. HOW IT FITS BANANA WORLD

The story the world already tells is about a place someone abandoned: your quest put you on Plot 11, empty since 1999, with a name scratched out of the registry — and The Cut is "a pit behind a house that got left." Same year. Plausibly the same person. The farm and the pit become two halves of one arc: first you make warm what they left cold — fire lit, troughs full, a name back in the book — and then, if you choose, you go down to face what they left behind, wearing armor your own farm made, with a lunch your own kitchen packed. Old Peel tells it. The farm never needs its own trophy shelf: your knitwear is seen at the rave, your produce will be wanted in Banana Town, your dog walks beside you everywhere. Everything the farm makes gets its audience somewhere else — which is how this world has always worked.

## 8. WHY PLAYERS WILL LOVE IT

- **"A hen followed me home, I named her Henrietta, and now she meets me at the gate."** Being recognized, by name, by something you chose to care for. That sentence is the whole game and players will say it to friends.
- **"My sheep made my armor."** No other cozy game lets your livestock's wool end up on your body in a nightclub, and later cast in bronze in a ghost pit. Provenance you farmed, seen by strangers.
- **Zero guilt, ever.** Nobody dreads opening this app. Nothing decays, nothing dies, two weeks away costs nothing, and the game says it missed you. Other farm games sell machines to delete their own care chores — we never build the chore.
- **Your farm is a self-description.** "Mine's a wool farm." "Mine's a cheese farm." The same ownership feeling that made people place 87 fence pieces, but now the fence decides something.
- **It respects a phone.** Two taps, some pleasant walking, ninety seconds, and tomorrow is always visibly loaded when you leave.

## 9. THE ORDER WE BUILD IT

**Slice 1 — Chickens (ships first, must be fun alone).** A hen follows you home. Two free hens, eggs overnight, the trough tap, the shimmer, the fed-double bonus, walk-over pickups, stall sales. A complete tiny farm with no signs, no roads, no promises.

**Slice 2 — The hug and the name.** Tap an animal, heart floats, bond climbs. Naming at bond three, and the away-news says her name back to you. This is the love slice.

**Slice 3 — Pens and the first choice.** Fence-built pens unlock the goat and the sheep. Wool day, milk, the second-pen decision, and the gate signs appear — showing only what's reachable today.

**Slice 4 — The cow and the kitchen.** The big pen, the cheese machine, the shelf that fills over days, dishes cooked from your own milk. The rooster joins: morning news plus three-day absence coverage.

**Slice 5 — The dog.** Bond ladder to the top: it meets you, follows you in the yard, and finally follows your banana into other areas.

**Slice 6 — The wool bridge.** Wool becomes knitwear you can wear at the rave. (Casting in metal waits for The Cut and appears on the sign only when the pit actually opens.)

One hard rule carried from the start: if the chicken slice doesn't prove people come back for eggs after a month, slices 3–6 don't ship into it. Chickens-only must stand on its own.

## 10. OPEN QUESTIONS FOR TRYM

1. **The golden signal.** The fed-today marker and the earned permanent golden animal can't both be the golden sprite — a one-tap bonus would counterfeit a two-week achievement (same logic as protecting the paid top hat). **Recommendation:** fed-today = a shimmer effect; the golden *sprite* is reserved for the permanent Golden Flock graduation. Yes/no?

2. **Where does knitting live?** Inside the forge (fits the "forge makes wearables" story, but the forge is nearly out of room) or as fixed recipes on the farm's own tailor table (drawn in the pack, cheaper, but less forge identity)? **Recommendation:** the tailor table on the homestead — ship the road sooner, move it to the forge later if you want.

3. **The rooster's insurance.** Extending stored yield from two days to three is a real gift to lapsed players, but it's your call whether absence coverage should be buyable at all. **Recommendation:** yes — it's cheap, characterful, and the kind of kindness this world is known for.

4. **Rumors about the pit.** Should the morning news occasionally whisper about the boarded-up house before The Cut exists? **Recommendation:** not one line until The Cut is actually live — then rare seasoning, never a weekly drumbeat. A promise on a schedule becomes a lie the moment the schedule slips.

5. **Naming twins.** Can two goats both be named Gunnar? **Recommendation:** one name per species per farm — it keeps "Gunnar remembers you" clean when you re-buy.
