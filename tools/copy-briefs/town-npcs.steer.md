## A line has to make sense to somebody standing in the square

On 14 Sep I tapped Dot and read: *"Trym Stene, have you noticed how every want points
somewhere?"* — "what does that even mean?" It means nothing to a player. A greeting is not
a riddle and not a mood. When a resident uses my name for the first time (rung 2) they say
one plain, concrete thing — about the square, their counter, the thing in their hand — the
way a person you have met twice actually talks. Dot may talk in questions; they must still
be questions with a plain meaning, about something a player can see.

## The two buttons must fit wherever the resident is standing

Under the line sit two buttons in the PLAYER's voice. The first asks what the resident is
doing. It is pressed at ANY hour, at ANY station — by the fountain, on a bench, crossing
the square — so it must never name an activity the player cannot see. Dot's said *"What
are you writing down?"* while Dot stood in the middle of the square holding nothing:
"nothing about him says writing". Write the button so it is true anywhere: *"What are
you up to?"*, *"What's keeping you busy?"* — plain, and the character's job may colour the
ANSWER, never the question.

## The station lines are about what they are visibly doing there

A resident's line at a station is a thought from the middle of what they are doing AT THAT
STATION — the bench, the counter, the fountain — not from their job in general. If the
station is a bench, the line is a bench line.

## Everyone speaks in the first person

A resident says "I keep the wants at the kiosk", never "Dot keeps the wants". The one
exception is Spinner's tic from the bible — he slips into the third person sometimes, and
only he does.
