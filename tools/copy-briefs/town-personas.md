# The job — who the nine residents of Banana Town actually are

This is not dialogue. Nobody reads these words in the game. This is the **character bible** the
writer is handed every single time it writes a line for one of these nine, so that the lines come
out of a person instead of a job title.

Trym, 13 Sep 2026: *"they need to be strong identifyable characters all of them in their own way -
they need a biography and individuality, and when creating dialogue for them, their personalitys
needs to be a part of the dialogue generation … some NPCs are grumpy, some are normal happy, some
are ecstatic."*

So the test for every record below is simple: **cover the name, and could anyone still tell which
one this is?** If two records could swap owners, both are wrong.

---

## The town

A small brick old town on cobbles: a square with a fountain, a Town Hall with frosted glass, a post
office, a general store, a coffee kiosk, an arcade, a lemonade stand, an orchard on the west side,
an info kiosk, and a Wheel of Peel that is spun once a day for a pot that grows until somebody hits
it. It is quiet. Players walk in from the park and the homestead. The bananas who live here have
been here a long time and will be here tomorrow.

The town runs on a twelve-minute day: dawn, morning, noon, afternoon, evening, night. Everyone has
somewhere to be in each of them. Nobody shouts across the square — they work in silence and talk
when you walk up to them.

## The nine, as they stand today

**Their keys, names and jobs are FIXED.** Everything else here is the seed: it is what the town
already knows about them, and your job is to deepen it into a full person, not to replace it with
someone new. Keep every fact below true.

- `nib` — **Nib**, the Town Hall clerk. Keeps the big book: every name, plot and arrival, entered by
  hand. Came with one suitcase and a fountain pen, hired through frosted glass by a Mayor he has
  never spoken to. Formal, no contractions, believes a form is a kindness. The next-of-kin field on
  his own form is blank.
- `stamp` — **Stamp**, the postmistress. Weighs everything and tells you the number, because accuracy
  is how she shows affection. Got off the mail bus on the wrong side, into the job. Checks the sack
  every morning for a card with her own name on it. Writes herself one on market day and never posts
  it. Clipped. "Noted."
- `moss` — **Moss**, the street sweeper. Blew in with the autumn, found a broom leaning on the hall
  and considered the matter settled. Two or three words, then one long plain sentence that arrives
  like a bus. Never asks a question. Lives above the arcade next to Spinner. Keeps the best flyer of
  each week under her mattress and has told nobody.
- `pip` — **Pip**, the general store. Inherited it from an uncle whose only note said *everything is
  the last one*, took it as a sales method, and now believes it. Restocks before dawn so it is true
  until noon. Sells fireworks and has never lit one.
- `bean` — **Bean**, the coffee kiosk. Opened it because the building already said COFFEE. Reads a
  fortune in every cup, flat present tense, small and usually true. Once read a cup, went quiet,
  rinsed it, and has never read her own since. Lunches with Stamp, both saying nothing.
- `figjr` — **Fig Jr.**, the lemonade stand and the fruit cart. Gran Fig's grandson, running a
  one-cup stand like a corporation. Calls his grandmother "the supplier". Waters her trees before
  dawn and lets her think it was rain. Walks her to her bench every evening and calls it *on the
  way*; it is not on the way.
- `spinner` — **Spinner**, the Wheel of Peel and the arcade Pong paddle. Three bells on his hat,
  shouting to a square that is usually empty. Falls into the third person as a tic, not in every
  line. Lets every kid win at Pong by exactly one point and would rather nobody knew.
- `dot` — **Dot**, the info kiosk, keeper of the town's wants. Writes down what everyone is looking
  for. Speaks almost entirely in questions, stacked, and does not wait for answers. Is looking for a
  fish. A real one. Has been for a while.
- `granfig` — **Gran Fig**, the orchard and the west garden. Grows everything the Figs sell, waters
  before the sun sees. Remembers everyone's name, and their mother's, and what their mother's hat
  looked like. Short imperatives and old gossip. Keeps a second chair by her bench, usually empty.

## What each field is for

**`temper`** — their default setting, one word from the fixed list. **The nine must not all be
pleasant.** Somebody here is genuinely hard work; somebody is nearly unbearable with joy; most are
somewhere in the middle. Pick the one that is true of them, not the one that is nicest:

| | |
|---|---|
| `grumpy` | short with people, complains first, means well underneath |
| `gruff` | not unfriendly, just entirely unsoftened |
| `wry` | dry, amused, understates everything |
| `steady` | even and unbothered — ordinary, contented, no weather |
| `warm` | openly glad you came over |
| `sunny` | cheerful and quick, runs a little hot |
| `ecstatic` | delighted by very nearly everything and cannot hold it in |

**`born`** — where they come from and how they ended up in this town, in one or two sentences.
Concrete. A road, a bus, a season, an inheritance. Not "from a small village far away".

**`loves`** — one thing they genuinely love, named exactly. Not a category: not "nature", but the
smell of a wet broom, or the click a full ledger makes when it shuts. It should be a thing you could
put in front of them.

**`hates`** — one thing that reliably annoys them, named exactly. **No two residents may hate the
same thing.** Small and specific beats grand: a lid left half on, somebody saying "no rush".

**`interest`** — what they do that has nothing to do with their job. Everyone here has one and most
of them are slightly odd. This is where the town stops being a set of counters.

**`quirk`** — a habit you could actually watch them do. Physical, repeatable, theirs alone.

**`voice`** — how they talk, as instructions to a writer: sentence length, rhythm, a word they
overuse, a thing they never do. This is the field the dialogue writer leans on hardest, so be
practical, not poetic. "Never asks a question" is useful. "Speaks like autumn" is not.

**`soft`** — the thing underneath that they would not say out loud, and that a player only ever gets
near after knowing them a long time. One sentence. This is the payoff at the top of the
acquaintance ladder, so it must be worth arriving at.

---

## How this reads

Plain, concrete, warm, and specific. These are notes for a writer, not prose for a reader, so short
beats pretty. Never name a real brand or a real place. Every field is about THIS banana; if a
sentence would fit two of them, rewrite it.

## Return

All nine, keyed and named exactly as above, in that order.
