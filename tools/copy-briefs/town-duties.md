# Banana Town — the work note (what your job wants from you this week)

Since 21 September a player can hold a job in Banana Town: at the General Store (Pip), at the
Arcade (Spinner) or at the Coffee Cup (Bean). One job at a time. The store and the arcade are
**payslip jobs**: they pay a cheque every Monday, delivered as a payslip in the letterbox at the
player's homestead. The Coffee Cup is a **tips job**: tips per cup, at the end of a shift, never a
cheque.

## The week's work (22 September)

A payslip job has **the week's work**: a short list of duties, each with a target for the week.
The cheque is the full rate scaled by how much of that work got done — do nothing, get nothing,
and the payslip prints the counts so the reason is on the paper. Trym: *"Arcade: Swept floor 0/3,
fixed Arcade machine 0/3, or something, you get the idea, and if all is ignored for a certain time,
that affects the payslip."*

- The Arcade: **the floor swept** (litter on the arcade floor, picked up) and **machines fixed**
  (a cabinet gone dark, brought back), three of each a week.
- The General Store: **the shelf restocked** (a crate from the back carried to the till) and
  **turning up** (days you were there), three of each a week.
- The Post Office, later: **the post sorted**.

If nothing at all has been done by Thursday, the boss writes a letter asking if you're coming in.
Two finished weeks with nothing done and the boss lets you go — asking again gets you the job back.

## The chip

The **work note** is a small paper in the corner of the square, the sibling of the quest's yellow
journal chip, in the town's brown-and-cream paper. It shows TWO lines:

1. **The counts** — composed by the game from the duty labels below and the numbers, e.g.
   *the arcade · floor swept 1/3 · machines fixed 0/3*. The labels are `kinds.*`: two or three
   lower-case words naming the duty AS DONE, under 18 characters, no numbers:
   `kinds.sweep`, `kinds.fix`, `kinds.restock`, `kinds.days`, `kinds.sort`.
2. **The line under the counts**, one of:
   - `wage` — the week's wage so far and the days to payday. MUST contain `{coins}` and `{days}`
     exactly once each; the game prints the numbers. Payday is Monday.
   - `done` — the week's targets are all met; the rest of the week is yours. No numbers.
   - `nudge.condo` / `nudge.store` — the boss has written to ask if you're coming in (Spinner at
     the arcade, Pip at the store): the week is slipping and nothing has been done yet. Warm, dry,
     a little pointed, never a threat. No numbers.
   - `fired.condo` / `fired.store` — the boss let you go after two empty weeks; his door is open
     if you ask again. Never cruel, never a lecture. No numbers.
   - `payslip` — a cheque has been paid and the payslip waits in the letterbox at home.
   - For the Coffee Cup only: `duty.cafe` (clock in at the serving window and make cups) until you
     have clocked in today, then `cafeDone` (tips gather on the tray as you pour, paid when you
     step away).

## Rules

- A NOTE TO YOURSELF, the same grammar as the quest chip: **lower case first letter**, short
  (under 70 characters; `wage` and the boss lines may run to 80), a phrase or one plain sentence.
- The one place in this world where an instruction belongs — but never a control: no "tap",
  "click", "button", "menu", "screen".
- **No numbers** in the prose, ever. `{coins}` and `{days}` are the only numbers, and only in `wage`.
- Never name the money as a reward, a bonus or a prize. A wage is a wage; a tip is a tip.
- Warm, dry, plain. A 13-year-old and a 50-year-old both read it without a stumble.


## The post office (22 Sep 2026)

Stamp hires now, and the post office is a payslip job like the store: its counts are `kinds.sort`
(rounds of sorting at the counter) and `kinds.days`. Two more boss lines, in exactly the grammar of
Spinner's and Pip's:

- `nudge.post` — Stamp, the postmaster, has written to ask if you're coming in; the pile on the
  counter is his subject. Warm, dry, a little pointed, never a threat. No numbers.
- `fired.post` — Stamp let you go after two empty weeks; his door is open if you ask again. Never
  cruel, never a lecture. No numbers.
