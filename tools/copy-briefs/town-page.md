# Banana Town — the page itself, now that it is the front door

Until 21 Sep 2026 Banana Town was a hidden prototype: noindexed, in no sitemap, linked from
nowhere. Its page said so out loud — *"the square, as a prototype. Not linked from anywhere."* —
and its browser title was *"Banana Town — prototype"*.

That is over. **Banana Town is where Banana World begins.** Every "Enter Banana World" link on the
site lands here, the page is indexed, and the story's first chapter opens at the fountain: Nib the
town clerk is waiting there for whoever walks in. From the square, roads and the travel door lead
to the park, Banana Bay, the homestead and the rave.

This job is the four lines of the page that are NOT spoken by anybody in the world. They are read
by a person deciding whether to walk in — on a search result, on a tab, and on the page above and
below the square.

## What is on the page

A pixel-art town square: brick fronts on cobbles, a fountain in the middle, a town hall at the top,
a general store, a post office, a coffee kiosk, an arcade, a garden, benches, streetlights. Nine
residents walk their own days through it in silence. The player's banana walks in from the south,
by tap or arrow keys, and taps things: a door opens a shop, a sign is read, a resident turns and
talks when you walk up to them. There is a town-health meter at the bottom. Nights come every few
minutes and bring ghosts; some nights are worse. Everything is free and plays in the browser.

## The four lines

- `title` — the browser tab and the search result's headline. It **must start with the two words
  "Banana Town"** (a counter on the desk matches page titles by that prefix — see WORLD_TITLES).
  After that, a dash and a few words that say what it is: a free pixel-art town you walk around in,
  the start of Banana World. Under 60 characters.
- `description` — the search result's grey line. One or two plain sentences, under 155 characters:
  what you do there, that the story starts here, that it is free and in the browser. No exclamation
  marks, no "welcome to".
- `tag` — the small line directly under the big BANANA TOWN sign above the square. Lower case, a
  phrase rather than a sentence, under 70 characters. It replaces *"the square, as a prototype. Not
  linked from anywhere."* — it should say, in the world's own voice, that this is where Banana World
  begins. It may name the fountain or Nib.
- `note` — the one line under the square that tells a newcomer how to play, under 180 characters.
  It replaces *"Walk with a tap or the arrow keys. Tap a door, a sign or a banana to read what it will
  be. The road south goes back to the park."* Keep the useful facts: walk by tapping or with the arrow
  keys; tap a door, a sign or a resident; the roads lead out to the rest of Banana World. Drop
  everything about a prototype. This is the ONE place on the page where an instruction belongs.

## The rules

1. Nothing about a prototype, construction, "coming soon", "under development", "beta". The town is
   simply open.
2. No numbers: not how many residents, shops, games or players. The world never publishes its own
   numbers.
3. Plain words a 13-year-old and a 50-year-old both read without a stumble. No "immersive",
   "experience", "vibrant", "cozy", "charming", "explore", "discover", "unlock", "adventure awaits".
4. "Banana World" and "Banana Town" are the only proper nouns that need to appear; "Nib" may.
5. The title and description are read out of context by strangers; the tag and note are read by
   somebody already looking at the square. Write each for where it is read.
