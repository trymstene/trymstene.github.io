# Banana Town — what the other places say when tapped

Six short lines for the places in Banana Town that, until 22 Sep 2026, still answered a tap from a
sentence typed into the code — three of them ending "Not built yet.", which is a developer talking,
not a town. The Town Hall, the bank and the print shop answer with a toast when tapped; the Wheel of
Peel and the Exchange open a card whose heading is their name and whose first line is yours; an old
arcade cabinet answers when tapped inside the arcade.

## ⭐ The one rule: a place answers plainly

Trym, tapping the lemonade stand: *"i dont understand any of this text, i dont understand what its
trying to say? clear and concrete messages like this please."* A PLACE is not a character. Its line
is a signpost and answers two questions, in this order, in plain words:

1. **What is this?** Name the place, and the banana who runs it if one does.
2. **What can I do here?** The thing a player can actually do — or that there is nothing to do here
   yet, said plainly and without apology.

No scenery, no metaphor, no riddle, no weather. Two plain sentences beat one pretty one. Never a
number (no price, no odds, no date), never a question, never "coming soon" with a promise attached.

## The fields

- `hall` — the Town Hall: Nib keeps the big book of every resident inside; nothing to do inside yet,
  and the story's second chapter will open it. Never say when.
- `bank` — the bank: it is a cash machine; nothing to do here yet.
- `print` — the print shop: the sticker packs stand in its window; nothing to do inside yet — the
  packs are sold in the site's shop, not here. No price.
- `wheel` — the line under the heading "The Wheel of Peel" on its card: Spinner's wheel. One free
  spin a day, then a few coins a spin; every paid spin feeds the pot, and one wedge takes the whole
  pot. "A few coins" is as precise as it gets; no odds.
- `exchange` — the line under the heading "The Exchange" on its card: Fig Jr. buys what your
  homestead made — eggs, milk, wool — at today's price, which moves from day to day. Sell now, or
  hold. No number.
- `oldCabinet` — an old cabinet along the arcade wall, tapped: it is out of order; nothing to play on
  it yet.
